# Monoliths
_A repository to host data and scripts exploring the 2025 and 2026 growth chamber experiments, simulating a summer heat wave and extended growing season on tundra monoliths._

## Description
The aim of this study is to investigate how a simulated heat wave and extended growing season impact the timing of plant senescence, above- and below-ground growth, and net ecosystem exchange in tundra samples. By isolating the effects of acute events (heat waves) from the lengthening of the potential growing season (i.e. lengthening period with temperatures where growth could occur), we aim to identify the physiological thresholds of tundra vegetation that determine the future of Arctic biomass and carbon sequestration. To address this, we will conduct a three-year growth chamber experiment to simulate a summer heat wave (T1) and an extended growing season (T2) and, compared to ambient temperatures (C), using intact tundra monoliths which include soil, intact roots and vegetation (n=21) collected from Qikiqtaruk - Herschel Island, Yukon. 

### Samples
The samples used in this experiment were collected from Qikiqtaruk-Herschel Island (QHI; approx: 69.591117, -139.075818), an ice-rich permafrost island located 5 kilometres off the north coast of the Yukon in the Beaufort Sea. QHI lies within the Inuvialuit Settlement Region and is part of Qikiqtaruk - Herschel Island Territorial Park, which is co-managed by the Inuvialuit and the Yukon Government under the Inuvialuit Final Agreement. QHI is a place of important natural and human heritage and it remains an active and a meaningful part of the Inuvialuit homeland.

In August of 2024, 21 tundra samples were collected from three active layer detachments (ALDs; landslides that occur when the top-layer of thawed permafrost, i.e. the active layer,  detaches from frozen permafrost soils beneath) on QHI. Samples included mixed-species tundra plants and intact roots and soil. The initial block samples were cut into 20cm diameter x 30cm depth cylinders (hereafter referred to as monoliths) in the field and were transported back to the University of British Columbia in Vancouver, BC in September of 2024.  

Following their arrival at UBC the monoliths were cared for until they senesced and then were stored in -20ºC freezers wrapped in snow blankets, to simulate winter dormancy where tundra plants would normally be covered in snow.  

### Experimental Runs

_**2025**_

In April of 2025, the monoliths were removed from the freezer, and repotted into 30cm diameter pots packed with sphagnum peat moss (Sunshine brand) in the outer rim around the original tundra soil. During repotting, six root growth cores were installed in the outer rim of each pot to measure root growth. 

Following re-potting the monoliths were moved to a climate controlled growth chamber to begin growth chamber experiments (where temperature and light conditions reflective of June 1st were simulated). In August 2025, after a growing season length of 128 days, the monoliths were returned to the -20ºC freezers wrapped in snow blankets.

In 2025, the treatments were as follows: 
1. Control (i.e. ambient temperatures derived from the 30-year average of the ECCC weather station on Qikiqtaruk).
2. Heat wave: Designed to simulate a typical growing season, with the addition of a two-week heat wave that reached 30°C and had an average daily temperature of 16°C. This was based on a real heat wave that occurred on Qikiqtaruk - Herschel Island in 2023.
3. Extended potential growing season (Early spring, and warm autumn): Designed to simulate a growing season which begins two weeks earlier than ambient (i.e. temperatures rise above 0°C two weeks earlier in the season, meaning there are essentially two extra weeks of 24-hours of daylight for the plants). Secondly, it also simulates a warm autumn where temperatures remain warm even as light begins to decrease.

_**2026**_

In April 2026, the plants were removed from the freezer in placed in the growth chamber, with temperatures simulating June 1st. Unfortunately, technical difficulties with the growth chamber forced us to move the plants back into the freezer after the plants experienced 3.5 days of above zero temperatures. The experiment was restarted on July 17th, 2026. 

In 2026, the treatments were as follows: 
1. Control: Same as 2025
2. Heat wave: Same as 2025
3. Extended potential growing season (Warm autumn only): Due to logistical constraints on the timing of the experiment, we were only able to simulate a warm autumn during the 2026 experimental run. This treatment involved an ambient spring and summer, followed by 14 days of summer temperatures, with decreasing daylight hours in autumn.
